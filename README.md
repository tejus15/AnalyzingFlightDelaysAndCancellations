PDX-DSM (Portland International Airport - Des Moines International Airport) route has the highest average departure delay.
Also, most of the routes with the highest mean departure delays originate from the Portland International Airport. 

### Possible reasons:
#### Weather:
Portland International Airport (PDX) is subject to the Pacific Northwest’s notorious wet weather. The city sees precipitation on roughly 156 days per year​, mostly during fall and winter. Heavy rain, low clouds, and frequent fog can slow down operations – dense fog often forms overnight and can persist into morning​, reducing visibility and causing delays or spacing requirements between flights.

#### Busy Periods at PDX: 
While not as large as LAX or O’Hare, Portland International is a significant airport with heavy traffic, especially during peak travel periods. The airport served a high volume of travelers as air travel rebounded in 2022–2023, and on peak days it can get quite crowded. During the holidays, for instance, PDX handled over 50,000 passengers per day around Christmas​. Even during spring break, TSA expected to screen about 25,000+ departing travelers on peak days in 2023 (about 15% above normal volumes)​. These surges can strain airport resources – security lines get longer, gates can become full, and scheduling on runways gets tighter. When many flights are trying to depart around the same time (early morning “pushes” or evening rush), PDX’s two runways and taxiways can become bottlenecked. Departing flights may have to wait in line for takeoff during busy banks of departures, which adds to delay times.

SEA-LAX (Seattle–Tacoma International Airport - Los Angeles Airport) route has the highest total cancelled flights. 
In fact, most of the routes with the highest cancellations originate from Seattle Tacoma International Airport.

Seattle-Tacoma International Airport saw a surge in flight cancellations in 2022 due to a combination of adverse weather, airline operational woes, personnel shortages, and infrastructure limitations. Based on the analysis above, the key factors can be summarized as follows:
## Severe Weather Impacts: 
Unusually intense winter weather events (snowstorms and an ice storm) in both early and late 2022 led to hundreds of cancellations at SEA (kiro7.com, businessinsider.com). Freezing rain in particular proved devastating, temporarily halting all flights and giving Sea-Tac the highest cancellation count of any U.S. airport on at least one day (businessinsider.com). These events highlighted the airport’s vulnerability to winter conditions and the time-consuming nature of safe de-icing operations.
## Airline Staffing and Operational Challenges: 
The airport’s largest carrier, Alaska Airlines, experienced a pilot staffing crisis in spring 2022, which caused an unusual number of flight cancellations (news.alaskaair.com). Training delays from the Omicron wave left Alaska with dozens fewer pilots than needed, forcing a 2% schedule reduction and many canceled flights (ktoo.org). 
Industry-wide crew shortages (pilots, flight attendants, etc.) and some airline-specific meltdowns (e.g. Southwest’s December collapse) added to Seattle’s cancellation tallies. Official statements from Alaska and others confirmed that staffing shortfalls were a primary driver of many cancellations (news.alaskaair.com, adn.com).
## Air Traffic Control Issues: 
Systemic ATC staffing shortages in the U.S. during 2022 created a backdrop of delays that occasionally translated into cancellations (globaltrademag.com). While Seattle did not suffer unique ATC outages, it was affected by nationwide constraints – for instance, when en-route centers had to slow traffic, or when low visibility required greater spacing between aircraft. A FAA report noted 77% of critical ATC facilities were understaffed in 2022 (globaltrademag.com), a factor that airlines say contributed to flight disruptions. Thus, ATC limitations served as a contributing factor to SEA’s cancellations, especially on high-volume days.
## Airport Infrastructure Constraints: 
Sea-Tac’s physical capacity was stretched thin in 2022. Limited de-icing equipment and pad space led to significant ground delays and some cancellations during winter storms (kiro7.com). Gate availability became a constraint during peak periods – arriving flights sometimes had to wait for gates, causing cascading delays (kiro7.com). With passenger volumes rebounding to ~46 million, the airport’s runways, gates, and support services often operated at or beyond designed capacity, leaving little margin when irregular operations hit. These infrastructure bottlenecks turned minor delays into major disruptions.
